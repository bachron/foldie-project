# Foldie Project: Brompton G Line (Upgraded) Build Plan

**Base bike:** used Brompton G Line, size S, bought as listed 18 Nov 2026 (listing and spec: `G_Line_Upgraded_Build.md`).
**Status:** in progress. Five upgrades chosen (below); more may follow.
**Separate from the HEXATI T20.** `T20_Build_Plan.md` and `T20_Build_Options.md` are unchanged. The T20 research is reused here as a menu of candidates, but only where it fits a real G Line.
**Rider priorities (carried over, confirm):** general city use, weight-conscious, Singapore sellers where possible. Child seats and their mounts are excluded from planning weights.
**Measured weight (owner, 21 Nov 2026, rough scale reading): the current G Line build is about 10 kg.** The earlier ~12.0 kg "Today" figure was my estimate and was about 2 kg too high. Method used to redo the savings: every old-part weight in the tables was scaled by 10/12 (0.83) because I cannot tell which parts were overestimated, then the new-part weight was subtracted. The new-part weights are published or quoted figures and were not scaled. Result: about **-1.5 kg (range -1.0 to -2.0 kg), finished bike about 8.5 kg (8.0-9.0 kg)**. Weigh each old part as it comes off and replace these rows with real numbers.

**Target weight:** not set yet. With the chosen upgrades the estimate is about **8.5 kg** (from the measured 10 kg).
**Weight today:** about 10 kg measured (owner, rough scale, 21 Nov 2026); the parts-list estimate of 10.9-13.1 kg (mid 12.0 kg) was too high. Full list: `G_Line_Current_Parts_List.md`. Stock G Line is ~14.8 kg (Brompton, 32.63 lb).

### Gearing: 54T chainring x 10-36T cassette (analysed 20 Nov, updated 21 Nov 2026)
Owner confirmed (21 Nov): 54T chainring with the 10-36T 12-speed cassette is the final choice. Tyres: now Schwalbe G-One 54-406, future Continental Contact Urban 40-45-406 (owner, 21 Nov). Rolling circumference is not published for every size: the Cateye table lists 40-406 at 1,490 mm, 47-406 at 1,515 mm and 50-406 at 1,565 mm and has no 54-406 row. I used about 1,590 mm for the 54-406 (extrapolated, estimate) and about 1,505 mm for a 42-406 (interpolated, estimate); both are good to roughly 1 percent. Cogs assumed to be 10-11-12-13-15-17-19-21-24-28-32-36 (SRAM XG-1270 10-36T layout).

| Cog | Gear inches now (54-406) | Gear inches future (42-406) | km/h at 90 rpm now | km/h at 90 rpm future |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 108 | 102 | 46.4 | 43.9 |
| 12 | 90 | 85 | 38.6 | 36.6 |
| 15 | 72 | 68 | 30.9 | 29.3 |
| 17 | 63 | 60 | 27.3 | 25.8 |
| 21 | 51 | 49 | 22.1 | 20.9 |
| 24 | 45 | 42 | 19.3 | 18.3 |
| 28 | 38 | 36 | 16.6 | 15.7 |
| 36 | 30 | 28 | 12.9 | 12.2 |

- The narrower tyre makes every gear about 5 percent lower: the same pedalling is about 5 percent slower and about 5 percent easier. The range stays 360 percent, steps of 8-14 percent.
- Cruising at 20-30 km/h at 80-90 rpm sits on the 15-21T cogs, the middle of the cassette, with either tyre.
- Top gear is 102-108 in (44-46 km/h at 90 rpm), more than a city folder uses.
- Lowest gear is 28-30 in, about the same as 34x34 on a 700c road bike. Fine for a light bike and flat Singapore riding. Much taller than the T20 plan's 48T x 11-50T (about 19 in), so expect it to be hard with a child seat and a child on a ramp or bridge. The 42-406 tyre makes it about 1.6 in easier.
- A smaller ring would move the range: with the 42-406, 50T gives 26 in low and 94 in high, 48T gives 25 in and 91 in. Ring availability and chain clearance not checked.
- Check: the Force AXS rear derailleur must be the version marked for a 36T largest cog (older Force eTap AXS derailleurs were limited to 33T). Chain length must be set for the 10-36T range with the tensioner.
