## Why Plan B is now the lead route

- Plan A's cost is too high. The stock carbon stem follows the T Line design, so matching G Line geometry (needed for the Thule Yepp Mini front seat) means paying for the Ti stem, about +S$500 in the Chris Yeo bundle.
- Plan B starts from a genuine G Line, which already has the right stem and geometry.
- Buying in London or Paris on the upcoming trip (used G Line). Ride it stock for a while, then sell the stock parts to fund each upgrade stage.

## Target spec: same as the T20

Every stage buys to the T20 spec in `T20_Build_Plan.md`, so parts move between Plan B and Plan A unchanged. That means:

- **Axles:** 100x12 front and 142x12 rear thru-axle. The SMC Plume DW2 wheelset is offered in 100/142.
- **Drivetrain:** derailleur setup (11- or 12-speed still open in the T20 plan).
- **Brakes:** disc, flat mount preferred.

You are treating the G Line as sharing the T20 axle standard. I could not find a published spec confirming the stock G Line rear spacing or axle type, and Wikipedia lists an 8-speed Alfine hub as stock gearing. Check both on the used bike. If it has the Alfine hub, stage 1 is a conversion: new freehub wheelset plus derailleur, shifter and cassette, with the Alfine wheel and shifter being what you sell.

## Other things to check

1. **Wheelset before fork and triangle.** Stage 1 wheels are bought before the stage 3 parts. Confirm the stock G Line dropouts take the wheelset's axle standard, or the wheels sit unused until stage 3.
2. **Priority 2 on the stock frame.** The crank, BB and chainring were picked for the T20 (M30 spindle, 110 BCD). Confirm they fit the G Line bottom bracket shell before buying.
3. **Used G Line condition.** Inspect on the trip: frame, hinges, folding mechanism, disc brake wear. Parts that are being sold later will fetch more if kept clean and complete.
